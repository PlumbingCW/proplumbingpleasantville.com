Plumber in Lakes Bay, Pleasantville, NJ

Lakes Bay is the bayside part of Pleasantville. The City's own flood study describes it as bordered by Lakes Bay on the east and by Ansley Boulevard on the north.

This page is for homeowners there who need a plumber or want to know what to check first. Tidal water and drainage shape most of the questions people ask here, so the page covers sump pumps, floor drains and what to do when water gets in. Call (609) 547-1417 and tell us your street.

Where Lakes Bay sits in Pleasantville

Streets named in the City's study include Ansley Boulevard, Bayview Avenue, Edgewater Avenue, East Greenfield Avenue, Oakland Avenue, East Park Avenue and Hampden Court. The study does not publish a block-by-block boundary.

Treat those streets as a guide and not a map line. If your home is on or near one of them, this page was written for you.

The older name Smith's Landing appears in the same study. It says Smith's Landing, one of the county's early settlements, was later subdivided in 1892 as the Lakes Bay subdivision.

Homes here were therefore built across many decades. That matters to a plumber only in one way: ask what year your house dates from, because parts and pipe type depend on it.

What the City says about flooding in this area

Pleasantville's flood information page says the part of the city south of the Atlantic City Expressway has tidal flooding from Lakes Bay, east of South Main Street. The repetitive loss study found that all 16 of the city's repetitive loss properties are in the Lakes Bay area.

It cites tidal and surge flooding along with poor drainage. For a homeowner, that means water in a basement or crawl space is not always a pipe problem, so find out whether the water came from outside before you pay for a plumbing repair.

What to do if water is coming into your home in Lakes Bay

Stay clear of electricity

Do not wade into water that could reach outlets or the panel. If the panel is wet, call your electric utility before anything else.

Find the source

If clean water is coming from a broken pipe, close the main valve. If water is seeping in through the floor or walls from outside, closing the valve will not stop it.

Protect what you can

Lift belongings off the floor and photograph the damage before you move anything, since your insurer will ask.

Flood coverage, permits and the City

Plumbing repair and flood coverage are separate matters. The City's flood page says its Construction Official and Zoning Officer can help you find out whether a property is in a Special Flood Hazard Area.

The page also notes that elevating a home or installing flood vents can lower flood insurance premiums. If you plan plumbing work below ground level, ask the construction official whether a permit is needed first.

Lakes Bay plumbing questions

Is Lakes Bay an official neighborhood of Pleasantville?

The City uses the name Lakes Bay for an area in its flood and planning documents. The documents we reviewed do not publish a formal neighborhood boundary, so we use the streets named in the City's study as a guide.

My basement floods during storms or high tides. Is that a plumbing problem?

Not always. Water that enters through the floor or walls from outside is a drainage and flood problem, while water from a broken pipe is a plumbing problem. A plumber can check your supply lines, drains and sump pump, but you may also need the City or your insurer.

How can I tell if my sump pump works?

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float rise and the pump start. If it does not start, or it runs without moving water, call a plumber before the next storm.

Who do I ask about flood zones and permits?

Ask the City of Pleasantville's Construction Official and Zoning Officer. They can tell you whether a property is in a Special Flood Hazard Area and whether your work needs a permit.
