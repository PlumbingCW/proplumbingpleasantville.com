A failed tank: what the emergency looks like

A tank that fails slowly drips from the base. One that fails suddenly can release dozens of gallons within minutes, which is why shutting off the cold supply matters more than finding the leak.

Once the water is stopped, the question becomes how fast you need hot water back. We can often remove the old heater and set the new one on the same day, depending on stock and the home's venting.

Signs the tank is finished

  • Water at the base of the tank rather than at a pipe fitting.
  • Rust streaks or flaking at the seams of the tank.
  • A unit older than about ten years that now needs repeated repairs.
  • Rumbling and popping that returns right after a flush.
  • Hot water that is rust colored from the tank itself.

Choosing the replacement

Sizing starts with how many people use hot water at the same time, not with the house's square footage. A tank that is too small runs out during back-to-back showers. One that is too large wastes energy keeping extra water hot.

Fuel is the next choice. A gas heater needs a proper vent and a gas line in good order. An electric unit needs the right circuit. Heat pump models need space and drain access. We explain each trade-off before anything is ordered.

Under New Jersey law, plumbing must be performed by a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor, or their W-2 employees, as the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers states. That is a good reason to check the credentials of anyone quoting a water heater swap.

Permit requirements for a replacement vary, so ask the Pleasantville construction office whether your swap needs one. We can describe what the job involves so the answer is quick.

What to do before we arrive

If the tank is leaking, close the cold water valve above the heater and switch off the gas or the breaker. Never touch a heater that is sitting in standing water with the power on.

Run a hot tap to confirm there is no hot water, then put towels around the base. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility and 911 before you call us.

Standards and sources

  • Plumbing work in New Jersey is governed by the Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15), which adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code. Your local construction official issues permits and inspects the work.

Water Heater Replacement questions in Pleasantville

How long does a replacement take?

A straightforward tank-for-tank swap is usually done within half a day. Changing fuel type or moving to a tankless unit takes longer because the gas, vent or electrical work changes.

Do I need an expansion tank?

Many homes with a closed water system need one to absorb thermal expansion. We check your supply pressure and tell you if your setup calls for it.

Can I replace it myself?

New Jersey's plumbing board says plumbing shall only be performed by a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor or W-2 employees. For gas units especially, a mistake in venting is a safety risk, so we recommend a licensed plumber.

What happens to the old tank?

We drain it, disconnect it and haul it away as part of the job.
