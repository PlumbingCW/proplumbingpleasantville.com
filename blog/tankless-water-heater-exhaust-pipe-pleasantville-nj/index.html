Tankless Water Heater Exhaust Pipe for Pleasantville, NJ Homes

The exhaust pipe is the part of a tankless installation that most often goes wrong. It carries combustion gases outside, so material, length, slope and clearance all matter for safety.

This guide explains how the vent works, what the installer must check and the mistakes we see most.

Home › Blog › Tankless exhaust pipe

Why the exhaust pipe matters

A gas tankless heater burns fuel and produces exhaust that contains carbon monoxide. The exhaust must leave the house through a sealed path, and any leak puts that gas indoors.

That is why the manufacturer's installation manual sets strict rules for the vent. Following them is a safety requirement, not a suggestion.

Vent materials

Common vent types
Vent typeUsed forNotes
Stainless steelNon-condensing unitsHandles higher exhaust temperatures.
PVC, CPVC or polypropyleneCondensing unitsOnly where the manufacturer lists it.
Concentric (pipe in a pipe)Condensing units with sealed combustionBrings in air and exhausts through one wall hole.
Existing chimneyRarely suitableUsually must be relined or avoided.

Never mix vent materials or use a pipe the manual does not list. A pipe that softens at exhaust temperatures can leak, and a leak is a carbon monoxide risk.

Use only the vent parts specified for your model, and seal every joint the way the manual directs.

Length, elbows and slope

Every manufacturer limits the total vent length, and each elbow counts as extra length. A run that is too long or has too many bends restricts the exhaust and trips the unit.

Condensing units also require the pipe to slope back toward the heater so condensate drains instead of pooling in the vent. A pipe that slopes the wrong way fills with water.

What the installer checks on the vent path

  • Total length and number of elbows against the manual.
  • Slope back to the unit for condensing models.
  • Clearance to windows, doors, meters and air intakes.
  • Support brackets at the spacing the manual lists.
  • Termination height above grade and snow.
  • Distance from the neighbor's property and walkways.

Where the vent ends

The exhaust terminal must sit at a safe distance from windows, doors and fresh air intakes, and above the height snow or flood water may reach. A terminal too close to a window can push exhaust into a bedroom.

Under New Jersey codes the local inspector confirms the termination. We confirm it again before we leave.

Carbon monoxide alarms

Install a working carbon monoxide alarm near the sleeping areas and near the heater. If it sounds, leave the house and call 911. Do not wait to see if it stops.

Common vent mistakes

The most common mistakes are the wrong pipe material, an unsupported long run, a pipe that does not slope and a terminal too close to a window. Each one can be avoided with a plan before installation.

If you buy a unit online and hire someone to hang it, ask them to read the manual with you before they start. A good installer welcomes the question.

Vent heights and Pleasantville flooding

The City of Pleasantville's flood information describes tidal flooding on its eastern and northern edges and flooding from Conovers Creek in the north. If your home is in or near those areas, place the vent terminal and the unit above the expected water level.

Ask the building department for the flood zone and base flood elevation on your property, and tell us when you call. We plan the vent height around that figure.

How tankless vent differs from a tank heater

A standard tank heater usually vents through a chimney or a draft hood. A tankless unit is a different design, and it needs its own vent.

If you are replacing a tank with a tankless unit, the old chimney vent often cannot be reused. See tankless water heater and water heater installation for how we plan the work.

What to do if the unit shows a vent error

Many units display an error code when the exhaust is blocked or the vent is too restricted. Do not reset it repeatedly. Turn the unit off and call.

Check outside for snow, leaves or a bird nest on the terminal. Clear nothing near a hot exhaust unless the unit is off and cool.

Inspecting an existing vent

If you already have a tankless unit, look at the vent from time to time. Check for loose joints, sagging pipe, staining at seams and any corrosion at the terminal.

If you see soot or a stain, turn the unit off and call. Do not tape over a leaking joint and keep running it.

Winter and exhaust terminals

Snow and ice can block a terminal. Keep the area clear after storms and check that nothing is stacked against the wall near the vent.

A blocked terminal causes error codes, and a unit that shuts down on a cold night is an emergency call.

Questions to ask before you install

Ask your installer

  • Which vent material does the manual require for this model?
  • What is the maximum vent length, and what is mine?
  • Where will the terminal sit, and how far from windows?
  • How will condensate drain?
  • Who confirms the installation with the local inspector?

When to replace an old vent

Vent pipe that is cracked, discolored or sagging should be replaced, not patched. Corrosion on a stainless vent or a soft spot on plastic means the pipe has reached the end of its life.

If you plan to upgrade the heater, replace the vent at the same time. A new unit on an old vent is a risk.

Why we read the manual on every job

Each model has its own vent rules, and manufacturers update them. What worked on the last install may not apply to the next unit.

We keep the manual at the job and check each rule against the actual run. That habit catches problems before they reach the inspector.

Keep the manual and the paperwork

Store the installation manual, the permit and the inspection record with your house papers. They matter if you sell or file a warranty claim.

Write down the vent length and the terminal location. A future plumber can read it in a minute and avoid guessing.

Standards and sources

  • Plumbing work in New Jersey is governed by the Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15), which adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code. Your local construction official issues permits and inspects the work.
  • Plumbers are licensed by the New Jersey State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. You can check a license with the board before work starts.
  • Gas appliances must follow the manufacturer's installation instructions and local code for venting, clearances and combustion air. Burner and gas work belongs with a licensed contractor.
  • Electrical work for outlets, switches and circuits must be done by a licensed electrician.

Questions from Pleasantville homeowners

Can I use PVC for any tankless vent?

No. Only use PVC where the manufacturer lists it for your model. Non-condensing units usually need stainless steel.

How long can a tankless vent be?

The manual sets the limit for your model, and elbows count toward it. The installer calculates it before work.

Does the vent need to slope?

Condensing models need the vent to slope back to the unit so condensate drains. Follow the manual.

Where does the vent end in a flood-prone area?

Above the expected water level. Ask the building department for the base flood elevation and give it to your installer.
