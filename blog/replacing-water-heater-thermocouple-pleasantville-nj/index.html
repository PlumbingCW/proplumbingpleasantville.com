Replacing a Water Heater Thermocouple in Pleasantville, NJ

A pilot light that will not stay lit is the classic sign of a worn thermocouple. This guide explains what the part does, how to tell it has failed and where the do-it-yourself line should stop.

If you ever smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility and 911 first. Everything else on this page can wait.

Home › Blog › Water heater thermocouple

What a thermocouple does

A thermocouple is a small metal probe that sits in the pilot flame of a gas water heater. The flame heats it and it produces a tiny electric current. That current keeps the gas valve open.

If the flame goes out, the current stops and the valve closes. This is a safety feature that prevents gas from flowing when nothing is burning, and it is why a failed thermocouple shuts the heater down.

Signs the thermocouple has failed

Common symptoms

  • The pilot light lights but goes out when you release the knob.
  • The pilot will not light at all.
  • The heater runs for a short time and then loses heat.
  • Soot or a weak, yellow pilot flame.
  • A heater that is more than ten years old with repeated pilot problems.

A weak flame or a dirty pilot can mimic a bad thermocouple. Cleaning the pilot orifice sometimes solves the problem, and checking the flame color is a good first step.

A steady blue flame that touches the tip of the probe is healthy. A yellow, lazy flame is not, and it can signal a draft problem or dirt rather than a part failure.

Where the do-it-yourself line should be

Replacing a thermocouple is within reach of a careful homeowner on a standard gas tank heater. It involves closing the gas, removing a small nut and fitting a new probe, then relighting the pilot.

Stop and call a plumber if you smell gas, if the heater is in a tight closet or if the heater has a sealed combustion or electronic ignition system. Those units have no thermocouple to replace in the usual way.

Close the gas

Turn the gas control to off and shut the gas supply valve. Wait several minutes for any gas to clear.

Open the access panel

Remove the burner access cover and locate the pilot assembly.

Remove the old probe

Loosen the nut at the gas valve and the bracket at the pilot, then pull the probe out.

Fit the new probe

Install the replacement in the same position and hand tighten, then snug with a wrench.

Relight and test

Follow the label instructions, hold the pilot button, then confirm the flame stays on when you release.

Check for gas first

Never relight a pilot if you smell gas. Open windows, leave the house and call the gas utility and 911. A plumber can replace the part after the utility confirms it is safe.

Gas, water heaters and Pleasantville homes

Whether your heater runs on gas or electricity, the age of the tank often matters more than the thermocouple. A heater near the end of its life may fail again within months.

New Jersey requires plumbing work to be done by licensed plumbers, and you can verify any plumber through the state's Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. Ask for the license number before work begins.

What to do while the heater is out

Your pipes will still carry cold water. If a leak or a gas smell is not involved, plan showers around the outage and keep the heater off until it is repaired.

If the cold weather is hard, the heater room may be near an exterior wall. Keep the door open so the room stays warm enough to protect nearby pipes.

Pilot problems that are not the thermocouple

A pilot that goes out in wind or when a door slams may have a draft problem, not a bad probe. A dirty pilot tube or a clogged orifice can produce a weak flame that fails to heat the probe.

A failed gas control valve is another cause. If a new thermocouple does not cure it, the control valve is the next suspect, and that is a job for a licensed plumber.

Safety habits around a gas water heater

Keep the area around the heater clear of boxes, rags and paint cans. Flammable vapors can drift to a pilot flame, and clutter blocks the air the burner needs.

Check the temperature setting once a year. A setting near 120 degrees reduces scalding risk and saves fuel, and it is easy to adjust on the gas control.

Signs it is time for a professional

Call a plumber if the burner flame is large, yellow or uneven, if the flue feels hot at the draft hood or if rust streaks run down the tank. Those signs point to a combustion or tank problem that a new probe will not cure.

A heater that needs attention every few months is telling you it is near the end of its life. Our water heater replacement team can walk you through the options.

Questions from Pleasantville homeowners

How much does a thermocouple cost?

The part itself is inexpensive. The price of a visit depends on the heater and the access, and we quote a clear price before work starts.

Can a thermocouple fail while the pilot is lit?

Yes. A worn probe can lose its output even with a flame. The pilot goes out when you release the button because the probe no longer holds the gas valve open.

How do I verify a plumber's license in New Jersey?

The State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers lists licensed plumbers. Ask your plumber for the license number and check it before work starts.

Does a new thermocouple fix a heater that smells of gas?

No. A gas smell needs the gas utility and an emergency response first.
