When to call an emergency plumber

Call an emergency plumber when water will not stop, when sewage backs up, when there is no hot water in cold weather or when a leak is near electrical equipment. Close the main valve first, then call the 24/7 line so a dispatcher can send help.

Not every plumbing problem is an emergency. A slow drain, a dripping faucet or a running toilet can wait for a scheduled visit, and we say so plainly when you call. If you are unsure, describe what you see and the dispatcher will help you decide.

Call the 24/7 line right away for

  • A burst or spraying pipe, see burst pipe repair.
  • Sewage in a tub, shower or floor drain, see sewer line cleaning.
  • No hot water from a gas or electric heater, see water heater repair.
  • A sump pump that stopped during a storm, see sump pump repair.
  • Water near the electrical panel or outlets.

Pleasantville homes and their plumbing

Pleasantville covers 7.28 square miles of Atlantic County, and more than a fifth of that is water. The city sits at about seven feet above sea level, and its own flood information describes tidal flooding on the east and north and creek flooding in the north. When storms arrive, that means sump pumps, floor drains and sewer lines work hard.

The city counts 7,856 housing units across neighborhoods such as Mount Pleasant, Risleyville and Smiths Landing. Homes of different ages carry different pipe, so a plumber who asks about the age of the house before arriving brings better parts.

New Jersey American Water serves the city from groundwater wells, and its 2022 report listed lead at 1 ppb and copper at 0.286 ppm at the 90th percentile, both below action levels. Water quality inside a house still depends on the pipes, which is why older homes deserve a look.

Plumbing work in New Jersey follows the plumbing subcode of the Uniform Construction Code (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15), and plumbers are licensed by the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. Ask your plumber for a license number and check it before work starts.
