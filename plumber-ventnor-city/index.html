Flooding and backups come first on the island

Ventnor sits about three feet above sea level, and the state's post-Sandy planning report for Ventnor says routine storm flooding reaches Ventnor Avenue and streets between Newark and Fredericksburg Avenues. Ventnor Heights on the bay side is named as especially vulnerable.

When floodwater rises, sewer and drain lines can back up into the lowest part of a house. A backwater valve and a working sump pump are the practical defenses, and our sewer line cleaning page explains the main-line side.

Island housing and vacancy

The city counts 7,897 housing units and almost half of them were vacant in 2020, which suggests many second or seasonal homes. A house that sits empty for weeks can leak unnoticed.

If you own a seasonal home, close the main valve when you leave and ask a neighbor to check the house after storms. A meter test through leak detection finds hidden loss when you return.

After a storm in Ventnor

Once water recedes, check the floor drain, the water heater and any pump before you use water again. Salt and silt can foul valves and drains, and a pump that ran for hours may be worn.

Photograph everything before you clean, and keep damaged items until your adjuster has seen them. Flood damage is usually separate from a standard homeowner policy, so ask about your coverage.
