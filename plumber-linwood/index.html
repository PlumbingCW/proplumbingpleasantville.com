What a Leedsville-era house means for your plumbing

Linwood was called Leedsville until 1880, and it became a borough in 1889. Its historic district spans 111 acres and 129 contributing buildings, so a good share of homes here are old enough to have had their plumbing changed more than once.

In a house like that, assume the visible pipe and the pipe in the wall may be different materials. A visual check in the basement tells us which, and it shapes whether you repair or plan a repipe.

Linwood has 2,813 housing units and a median resident age near 46. Homes that owners have kept for decades usually have a water heater that has been swapped once and supply lines that have not.

If yours is among them, ask us for an inspection before you remodel a kitchen or bath, because the walls are open and plumbing is cheapest to change then.

Water, wells and mains

New Jersey American Water lists Linwood among the communities its Atlantic system serves, and that system drew on groundwater from 23 wells. Water from the system is treated, but what happens inside your house depends on your pipes.

If you want to know what your pipes are made of, ask us to look at the pipe entering the house and at the water heater connections.

Remodeling in a Linwood house

Kitchen and bath remodels are the best time to replace old supply pipe, valves and drain sections. Once the walls are closed, the same work costs far more.

Ask us to look before the contractor starts, and we tell you what can stay and what should go. See plumbing installation for new fixtures and hookups.
