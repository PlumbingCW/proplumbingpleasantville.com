When a leak is an emergency

Active water through a ceiling, a wall that feels warm, or a floor that suddenly turns soft all call for an immediate shutoff. Close the main valve, switch off breakers serving wet rooms, and move electronics away from the area.

A slow leak is different. It shows up as a rising water bill, a meter that moves when every fixture is off, or a damp smell. Those are not emergencies in the same sense, but they cost money every day.

Symptoms that point to a hidden leak

  • The water meter keeps turning when every tap is off.
  • A water bill that has crept up with no change in use.
  • Warm spots on a floor or a hot slab smell.
  • Sound of running water in a quiet house.
  • Stains, bubbling paint or musty smells on a ceiling or wall.
  • Low pressure that appeared suddenly.

Meter still running: what to do first

Turn off every fixture, including the icemaker and irrigation, then watch your meter dial or low-flow indicator. If it moves, water is leaving somewhere. Closing your main shutoff and watching again tells you whether the leak sits inside the house or on the line from the meter.

That single test saves a lot of guessing. A leak that stops when the main closes is on your side of the valve. One that continues may be between the meter and the house, a case we describe on our water service line repair page.

Slab, crawl space and ceiling leaks

A slab leak means a supply line under a concrete floor is losing water. The usual clues are a warm patch, a running meter and the sound of water with every tap closed. Locating it before breaking the concrete limits the opening to the right spot.

Crawl space leaks tend to show up as damp insulation, mold smell or sagging floors above. Ceiling leaks may come from a supply line, a drain line or a failed fixture upstairs, so we trace the path before cutting drywall.

Irrigation systems and a yard line to a garden spigot can also leak outside the house. A pool or spa line follows the same approach. Water that appears in the yard but not in the house points to the underground line rather than the plumbing inside.

Local timing: tidal flooding versus a pipe leak

Pleasantville has tidal and storm flooding that can leave water in low yards and crawl spaces. A wet crawl space after a storm is not always a plumbing leak, and a meter test separates the two. If the meter is still, the water is coming from outside. If it moves, the leak is in a pipe.

Leak Detection questions in Pleasantville

How do I know if I have a leak or just a high bill?

Run the meter test. With every fixture off, a moving meter means water is escaping. A still meter points to a billing issue or a one-time use spike.

Can you find a leak without cutting into walls?

Usually, yes. Pressure testing, acoustic and thermal tools narrow the source so that only a small opening is needed, if any.

Will my water utility help with a leak adjustment?

Ask your water provider whether it offers any bill adjustment after a repaired leak. We can document the repair for you.

Do you handle repairs after you find the leak?

Yes. Depending on the source we move into burst pipe repair, repiping or a simple fitting replacement.
