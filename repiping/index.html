Signs it is time to repipe

When repairs stop making sense

  • Leaks in several places within a few years.
  • Brown or orange water when you open a tap.
  • Low pressure that spot repairs did not fix.
  • Pinhole leaks in copper.
  • Visible corrosion or flaking on exposed pipe.
  • A plumber finding galvanized pipe closing from the inside.

One leak is a repair. Three leaks in two years is a pattern. Each repair opens a wall, and the pipe next to the repair is as old as the one that failed.

We show you the failed section when we have it. A corroded interior is clear evidence, and we do not push repiping when a repair will hold.

What repiping involves

We plan the route, shut off the water for the working day, cut small access openings and run the new lines. Fixtures are reconnected and the system is pressure tested before the walls are closed.

Plumbers do not do drywall and paint. We tell you which openings we make so a finish contractor can patch them.

Older homes in established neighborhoods

Homes built before the 1960s often have galvanized steel or older copper with lead solder. If your home is in an established area, ask us to look at the pipe where it is exposed. A visual check at the basement or crawl space often tells us the material and the condition, and it helps you decide whether to repipe now or watch it.

Water quality context

New Jersey American Water's 2022 Atlantic system report lists lead at 1 ppb and copper at 0.286 ppm at the 90th percentile, both under action levels. Those figures describe the utility's system, not your pipes. If you are concerned about your plumbing, ask for an inspection, and a service line check covers the pipe outside.

Planning a repipe around your life

A repipe disrupts the house for a few days, so we plan it room by room. Water is shut off for part of each working day, and we tell you the hours ahead of time.

If you can, move valuables out of the walls we will open and clear the access points. A clear path shortens the job and lowers the cost.

What happens after the new pipe is in

We pressure test every run before any wall is closed. Then we reconnect fixtures, flush the lines and check pressure at each tap.

You get a plain explanation of what was replaced and what was left. Finish contractors patch the openings, and we tell them where each one is.

Repiping questions in Pleasantville

How long does repiping take?

A typical house takes a few days, depending on size and access. We give you a schedule before we begin.

Will I have water during the job?

Water is off for part of each working day. We tell you the hours ahead of time.

Can I repipe part of the house?

Yes. If only a bathroom or one floor is failing, we can replace that run and leave the rest.

Is repiping an emergency?

A burst or active leak is. Planning a repipe is not, and we can schedule it at a convenient time.
