About us

About Pleasantville Pro Plumbing

We connect Pleasantville homeowners and businesses with licensed plumbers on a 24/7 line.

Here is how we work and what to expect.

Call (609) 547-1417
Home › About

We run a 24/7 plumbing dispatch line for Pleasantville and nearby Atlantic County towns. When you call, a dispatcher listens, tells you what to shut off and sends a licensed plumber the same day when one is available.

How we work

We tell you the price before work starts, and we explain what the plumber found in plain terms. If a repair is not worth doing, we say so.

We do not promise an exact arrival time, because traffic and earlier jobs change it. We give you a realistic window and update you if it changes.

What you can expect from a visit

On every call

  • A dispatcher who tells you what to shut off.
  • A licensed and insured plumber.
  • A clear price before work starts.
  • A tested repair and a plain explanation.
  • Honest advice on repair versus replacement.

How to check any plumber's license

New Jersey licenses master plumbers through the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. You can check a license on the board's website before you agree to work.

Ask any plumber for the license number and confirm it matches the person standing in your home.

Where we work

We serve Pleasantville and the nearby towns of Absecon, Northfield, Egg Harbor Township, Linwood and Ventnor City. See the list of services for what we handle.

Calls may be recorded

We may record calls to the dispatch line to improve service and to document what was agreed. Our privacy policy explains how we use recordings.

What we do not do

We do not use contact forms, we do not promise exact arrival times and we do not promise outcomes we cannot control. We would rather tell you the limits of a repair than overpromise.

If a job is outside plumbing, such as a foundation crack or a gas burner problem, we say so and suggest the right kind of specialist.

Local knowledge

Pleasantville has tidal and creek flooding, older housing and a water system that draws on groundwater wells. We plan visits with those conditions in mind, which is why our town pages describe them.

Knowing the area helps us bring the right parts and ask the right questions when you call.

What we handle

Most calls involve drains, water heaters, leaks, toilets and faucets, sump pumps and sewer backups. We also take planned work such as water heater installs, repiping and new fixtures.

Each service has its own page that explains the problem, the repair and what affects the price, so you can read before you call.

Houses we see

Our towns have housing from historic districts to newer subdivisions. Older homes tend to need drain, valve and supply-pipe attention, and newer homes tend to need water heater and fixture work.

We ask about the age of the house when you call so the plumber arrives with suitable parts.

What this site is for

The service pages explain each problem, the repair and the price factors, and the town pages cover local conditions. The guides in the blog go deeper on topics such as sump pumps, tankless venting and leak detection.

Use them to prepare before you call, and call the dispatch line when you are ready or when something cannot wait.

If something goes wrong

If a visit does not go as expected, call the dispatch line and tell us. We would rather fix a misunderstanding than lose your trust.

Tell us what happened, what you expected and what you would like us to do, and we will respond.

Questions we welcome

Ask the dispatcher anything about the visit before you commit: what the plumber will check, what the price covers and what could change it. A clear answer now prevents confusion later.

If you want a second opinion on another plumber's quote, we are glad to explain what we would look at and why.

Pricing in plain terms

We do not publish a price list, because the right price depends on the house, the access and the problem. The plumber looks first and quotes a clear price before starting.

If the price changes because the plumber finds something different, we explain what changed and ask you before going ahead. Nothing proceeds without your agreement.

Why a dispatch line

Plumbing emergencies do not follow business hours. A line that answers at night lets us tell you how to stop the water immediately, which often prevents most of the damage.

The dispatcher then sends a licensed plumber the same day when one is available, and gives you a realistic arrival window.

Standards and sources

  • Plumbing work in New Jersey is governed by the Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15), which adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code. Your local construction official issues permits and inspects the work.
  • Plumbers are licensed by the New Jersey State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. You can check a license with the board before work starts.
