Plumber in Midtown, Pleasantville, NJ

Midtown is the name the City of Pleasantville uses for the area around downtown. Its 2022 revitalization strategy places it between Route 9 on the west, the Atlantic City Expressway on the north, Bayview Avenue on the south and the bay waterfront on the east.

What the City says about Midtown housing

The strategy counts 2,340 households and 7,085 residents across the four census block groups it covers, centered on the junction of Washington Avenue and Main Street. It reports that 52.4 percent of households rent, against 44.2 percent citywide.

It also reports more than 1,000 units built before 1940. A building's age tells you when it went up, not what pipe is inside, so a plumber has to look before saying anything about its condition.

Because so many Midtown homes are rentals, many plumbing calls start with the question of who pays. That depends on your lease and New Jersey tenant rules, so tell your landlord in writing first and keep a copy.

If you own, you choose the plumber and the timing. Ask for the repair to be explained in plain terms before work starts.

Vacant units and idle plumbing

The strategy reports a non-seasonal vacancy rate of 16.2 percent in Midtown, compared with 11.3 percent citywide. Empty units still have water in the pipes.

A hidden leak or a frozen line in an empty unit can damage the homes around it. Owners should shut off water to unoccupied units and have someone check them after storms and cold snaps.

Midtown plumbing questions

Is Midtown an official neighborhood?

The City uses Midtown as the name of a Neighborhood Preservation Program district, and its 2022 strategy defines the area by the boundaries above. Residents may use other names for parts of it.

I rent in Midtown. Who do I call about a leak?

Tell your landlord or manager first, in writing, because who pays depends on your lease and New Jersey tenant rules. You can call us for information, but do not authorize repairs to a rental you do not own.

Does an older building mean I need new pipes?

No. Age alone does not mean pipes need replacing. A plumber has to inspect the lines to say whether the material and condition are a problem.

What should owners of vacant units do?

Shut off water to units nobody uses and check them after freezes and storms. Look for stains, damp floors and the sound of running water.
