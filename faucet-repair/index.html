The valve will not close: what to do first

Look for the small oval handle under the sink. Turn it clockwise to stop the supply. If it spins freely or leaks when you turn it, that valve has failed, and you should shut off the main water valve instead.

The main valve is usually near the water heater, the meter or where the line enters the house. Tell us which one you used when you call.

Faucet and valve problems we fix

  • A drip from the spout that wastes water around the clock.
  • A handle that leaks or is hard to turn.
  • Low flow from a clogged aerator or worn cartridge.
  • A tub or shower valve that drips after shutoff.
  • Corroded supply lines or angle stops.
  • Outdoor spigots that leak inside the wall.

If the faucet is old and parts are hard to find, replacing the fixture costs about the same as repeated repairs. We tell you which path is smarter and do not push replacement on a faucet with a ten-minute fix.

Supply valves and tub drains

A shutoff valve that has not moved in years often seizes. Forcing it can snap the stem and release water. We replace angle stops and supply lines so the next emergency is a handle turn instead of a flood.

A tub or shower that drains slowly is usually a hair clog, so the fix sits in the drain rather than the valve. That belongs on our drain cleaning page, and a running toilet is covered under toilet repair.

Faucet Repair questions in Pleasantville

Is a dripping faucet worth fixing?

Yes. A steady drip wastes thousands of gallons a year and wears the seat further. Most drips are cartridge or washer repairs that take under an hour.

Why is the water pressure low at one faucet?

A clogged aerator is the common cause. If cleaning it does not help, the cartridge or the supply line is worn.

Can you repair a shower valve behind the wall?

Often through an access panel. If the valve body itself is corroded, we explain the options before opening the wall.

Do you replace outdoor spigots?

Yes, including leaks inside the wall. We also advise on winterizing them each fall.

Do you handle faucet emergencies at night or on weekends in Pleasantville?

Yes. A valve that will not close or a supply line that bursts can happen at any hour. The 24/7 line answers nights and weekends, we dispatch the same day when a plumber is available, and we tell you how to close the main valve first.
