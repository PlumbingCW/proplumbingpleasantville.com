Who Can Install a Garbage Disposal? A Pleasantville, NJ Guide

A garbage disposal touches plumbing and electricity, so the right installer depends on the setup. Replacing an existing unit is simple, and adding one where none exists is a larger job.

This guide explains who can do which parts and what to ask before you hire.

Home › Blog › Who installs a garbage disposal

The short answer

A licensed plumber can install a disposal and handle the drain, the dishwasher connection and the sink. If a new outlet or switch is needed, a licensed electrician does that part, and some plumbers coordinate both.

A handy homeowner can swap one disposal for another of the same type. A new install is harder, and a wiring mistake is a safety risk.

What a new install involves

Check the sink

The sink needs a standard drain opening and room below for the unit.

Plan the power

A nearby outlet on a proper circuit, with a switch you can reach.

Mount the unit

Fit the mounting ring and hang the disposal from it.

Connect the drain and dishwasher

Reconnect the trap and knock out the dishwasher plug if there is a dishwasher.

Test

Run water and check for leaks and noise.

Many plumbers will tell you to check the dishwasher knock-out plug. If it stays in place, the dishwasher cannot drain and water backs up.

A single-bowl sink and a double-bowl sink have different trap layouts, and the installer should check which you have.

Choosing the right unit

What to consider

  • Horsepower, with higher horsepower grinding tougher scraps.
  • Feed type: continuous feed or batch.
  • Noise insulation.
  • Stainless components versus plastic ones.
  • Septic compatibility if your home has a septic tank.

For most kitchens a continuous-feed unit of moderate horsepower is enough. A busy household that cooks daily does better with more power.

A disposal does not replace a trash can. Grease, fibrous peels and starchy foods still cause clogs, as our garbage disposal repair page explains.

Permits and licensing

New Jersey licenses master plumbers through the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, and you can verify any plumber's license with the board. Electrical work needs a licensed electrician.

Permit rules vary by town. Ask the local building department whether a disposal install needs one, or ask us to confirm.

Disposals and sewer service in Pleasantville

Many properties connect to a municipal sewer, but some homes in the wider area use septic systems. Check with your municipality, because many septic systems do not suit heavy food waste.

A disposal adds solids to the line, so older cast iron drains may clog sooner. If your drains are old, ask us to run a camera and consider drain cleaning before adding load.

What it costs to hire a pro

The price depends on the unit, whether an outlet is needed and how complex the drain connection is. We quote after we see the sink and the cabinet.

Ask whether the price includes haul-away of the old unit and any needed parts.

Replacing a failed disposal

If the old unit used the same mounting ring, replacing it is straightforward. Disconnect the power, drain, and dishwasher hose, release the ring, and hang the new unit.

Many disposals use a standard mount, but older models may not. Check the ring and plan to replace it if it is cracked or corroded.

Questions to ask before you hire

Ask the installer

  • Are you licensed, and what is the license number?
  • Does the price include the electrical work?
  • Will you check the dishwasher knock-out and drain?
  • What does the warranty cover?
  • Will you remove the old unit?

Signs of a bad install

A leak at the mount, a loose unit, a unit that vibrates or a drain that backs up after installation signals a bad install. Turn off the power and call the installer.

A properly installed disposal runs smoothly and leaves no moisture under the sink after a day of use.

Maintenance after installation

Run cold water while the disposal runs and for several seconds after. This flushes waste through the line and keeps grease from sticking.

Avoid putting fibrous or starchy foods in the unit, and clean it occasionally with ice and citrus peels or a manufacturer-approved cleaner.

Why a clean install matters

A disposal sits under the sink and shakes every time it runs. A loose mount causes leaks and noise, and a poorly sealed drain can drip into the cabinet.

Take time to check the gasket, the mounting ring and the drain connections after the first week. Tighten anything that has loosened.

Disposal alternatives

Some households skip the disposal and compost scraps instead. That keeps solids out of the line and reduces the chance of a clog.

If you rent or have older drains, composting may be a better choice. A disposal suits a household that wants the convenience and keeps grease out of the drain.

Planning around kitchen use

Tell the installer how you use the kitchen. A household that cooks heavily needs a different unit than one that eats out most nights.

Also mention any past clogs. A line that has clogged before may need cleaning before a disposal goes in.

Safety around a disposal

Never put your hand or a utensil in the unit, even when it is off. Switch off the breaker or unplug it before any work.

If something is stuck, use tongs or pliers and a hex wrench at the bottom to free the plate. Teach everyone in the house the same rule, including children and guests who may use the kitchen, so nobody reaches in while it is plugged in or running, and keep pets away from the sink.

Standards and sources

  • Plumbing work in New Jersey is governed by the Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15), which adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code. Your local construction official issues permits and inspects the work.

Questions from Pleasantville homeowners

Can a plumber wire a disposal?

In New Jersey electrical work needs a licensed electrician. Many plumbers bring one in for new outlets or switches.

Can I install a disposal myself?

Replacing the same type is realistic. A new install means drain and electrical work, and a mistake can cause leaks or shock.

Do I need a permit?

It depends on the town and the scope. Ask the local building department.

Can I use a disposal with a septic tank?

Check with the septic system maker or a septic professional first, because heavy food waste can strain some systems.
