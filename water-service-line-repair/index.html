Signs of a service line leak

What homeowners notice

  • Low or no water pressure through the whole house.
  • A wet patch, sinkhole or soft ground between the street and the house.
  • A water bill that jumps with no change in use.
  • A meter that spins with every fixture closed.
  • The sound of running water near the entry point.

Test it with the meter. Close every fixture, then check whether the meter dial or leak indicator still moves. If it does and the house shows no leaks, the loss is likely underground.

Mark the wet spot with a stake and take a photo. Where the line is leaking affects who is responsible for the repair.

Who owns which part of the line

In many systems the utility owns the main and the connection up to the meter or curb stop, and the property owner owns the pipe from there to the house. Ownership differs by utility and by town, so confirm with your water provider before digging.

New Jersey American Water serves Pleasantville. Call the utility if the leak sits near the meter or curb stop, and call us for the pipe on the house side. We can tell you which side we think the failure is on.

Repair methods

A trench repair opens the ground, cuts out the failed section and replaces it. It is the right choice when the leak is short and the yard is open, and we restore the soil afterward but not landscaping.

If the line is old or has failed more than once, replacing the whole run costs less over time than repeated partial repairs. Our repiping page covers pipe inside the house, and leak detection pins down where the line is leaking before anyone digs.

Lead and your service line

New Jersey American Water reported lead at 1 ppb at the 90th percentile in its 2022 Atlantic system report, below the action level. A lead service line is a separate matter from that average. If your home is old, ask us or the utility to identify your line material when it is exposed.

What to do before we arrive

Close the main valve inside the house and open the lowest faucet to drain the pipes. Mark any wet or sunken ground outside and take photos. Do not dig near the line yourself, and call 811 before any digging so buried utilities are marked.

Tell the dispatcher whether the water is out completely or just weak, and whether the wet area is near the meter. That helps us decide which side of the meter the leak is on.

Water Service Line Repair questions in Pleasantville

Who do I call first for a leak near the meter?

Call your water provider if the leak is on their side of the meter or curb stop, and call us for the house side. If you are unsure, call us and we will help you figure it out.

Can you find the leak without digging up my whole yard?

Yes. We use listening and testing methods to locate the leak so digging stays as small as possible.

How long does a service line repair take?

A short repair often finishes in a day. A full replacement takes longer, and we give you a time estimate before work.

Yes, for part of the visit. We tell you when to expect it so you can store water for the day.
